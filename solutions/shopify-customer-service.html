Managing Customer Service on Shopify

Everything you need to know about setting up, scaling, and optimizing customer support for your Shopify store — from common ticket types to automation strategies.

Ticket mix

The 5 Tickets Eating Your Inbox

If you run a Shopify store, you already know the pattern. The same questions land in your inbox day after day. Understanding these ticket types is the first step to automating them.

"Where Is My Order?"

WISMO tickets are the #1 support request for every e-commerce brand. They account for 40–60% of all inbound tickets and are almost entirely automatable.

Returns & Refunds

Customers want to return items, exchange sizes, or get a refund. Clear policies and streamlined workflows reduce handling time by 70%.

Product Questions

"Does this come in blue?" "Is this compatible with X?" Pre-sale questions directly impact conversion. Fast, accurate answers close sales.

Order Modifications

Address changes, cancellations, adding items — these require direct integration with Shopify's order API to handle efficiently.

Shipping Issues

Lost packages, delayed deliveries, wrong items. These require coordination with carriers and often need human judgment for resolution.

Tooling

Building Your Shopify Support Stack

Your support stack is the combination of tools that manage customer communications. The right setup depends on your volume, channels, and budget.

Helpdesk Options

Most Shopify stores use a dedicated helpdesk to centralize support. Popular options include Gorgias (built for Shopify), Zendesk, Freshdesk, and Intercom. Each pulls in emails, chat, and social messages into one queue.

If you're under 100 tickets/month, Shopify Inbox or even a shared Gmail might work. Once you cross that threshold, a proper helpdesk pays for itself in time saved.

Channel Strategy

Don't try to be everywhere at once. Start with the channels your customers actually use:

  • Email — still the highest volume for most stores. Customers expect a response within 4–12 hours.
  • Live chat — critical for pre-sale conversion. Even a simple widget increases conversion rates by 10–15%.
  • Social DMs — Instagram and Facebook DMs are growing. If you sell on social, support must be there too.
  • SMS — increasingly used for order updates and proactive outreach, less so for inbound support.
Order data first

Connect your Shopify order data to your helpdesk so agents (or AI) can pull up tracking, order details, and customer history without switching tabs. This alone cuts average handling time by 40%.

Routing

AI vs. Human Agents: When to Use Each

The right approach isn't "all AI" or "all human." It's knowing which tickets benefit from automation and which need the human touch.

Ticket Type Best Handled By Why
WISMO AI Repetitive, data-driven — AI pulls tracking info instantly
Return status AI Policy-based, can be fully automated with order lookup
Product questions AI (with human fallback) AI handles common questions; niche or technical ones go to humans
Complaints Human Emotional context matters — empathy can't be faked
VIP customers Human High-value relationships deserve personal attention
Billing disputes Human Requires judgment, negotiation, and accountability
The 80/20 Rule

For most Shopify stores, roughly 80% of tickets are repetitive and data-driven — perfect for AI. The remaining 20% require human judgment, empathy, or creative problem-solving.

Scaling

Scaling Without Drowning

Every Shopify store hits a scaling cliff where the inbox starts to win. Here's how to stay ahead of it:

Build a knowledge base first

Before you hire anyone or add AI, create self-service content. A well-organized FAQ page can deflect 20–30% of tickets before they're ever created.

Automate the repetitive stuff

WISMO, order status, return policy questions — these are perfect for automation. Set up auto-responses or AI agents for these categories first.

Add human support strategically

Don't hire a full-time agent until you're consistently above 500 tickets/month. Below that, consider part-time, outsourced, or AI-first approaches.

Monitor, iterate, repeat

Review your metrics weekly. Identify new repetitive ticket patterns and automate them. Your goal is to keep humans focused on the 20% of tickets that truly need them.
